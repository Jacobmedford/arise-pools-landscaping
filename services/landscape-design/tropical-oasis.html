Resort-Style Planting

The Resort Feeling, at Home

A tropical oasis surrounds the pool with palms, layered green and bright color, so the yard feels like a getaway the moment you step outside.

The look comes from layers. Tall palms set the canopy and cast moving shade, mid-height shrubs and grasses fill in, and flowering color and groundcover soften the edges. Turf or a lawn panel adds cool green underfoot, and uplit palms carry the mood into the evening.

The challenge in Arizona is choosing plants that look tropical and still handle summer afternoons well past 110 degrees, alkaline soil and the occasional frost. Mediterranean fan palms, date palms, red bird of paradise, lantana, hibiscus in the right exposure and bougainvillea on a warm wall deliver the feel without a constant fight.

We concentrate the lushest planting where you see and use it most, around the pool and patio, and transition to tougher plants near the walls. That keeps water use reasonable and the oasis healthy in August. Lagoon-style freeform pools suit this style naturally.

Built for Arizona

Lush Planting, Desert Rules

A tropical look in the Valley depends on honest plant choices and smart water.

Heat

Afternoon protection

Many tropical-looking plants scorch in full western sun. Placing them where buildings, palms or shade structures block the late-day heat keeps leaves green through summer.

Frost

Winter cold snaps

Bougainvillea, hibiscus and some palms can be damaged on the coldest nights. Warm walls, mixed planting and frost cloth on forecast frost nights protect the most tender plants.

Soil

Alkaline ground

Valley soils are alkaline, and some palms, including queen palms, often struggle to take up nutrients in them. We favor palms suited to our soil and amend the lush beds.

Pool litter

Beauty with less cleanup

Bougainvillea drops bracts and some palms shed fronds and fruit. We keep the messiest plants back from the water and choose cleaner ones for the pool edge.

The Process

Planting an Oasis That Lasts

Map sun and shade

At the site visit we note which walls get afternoon sun, where frost settles and where the pool needs shade.

Layer the design

Palms, shrubs and color are layered around the pool in a 3D design delivered inside a week.

Prepare the beds

Soil is amended and drip zones are run so lush beds get more water than the tougher plants beyond them.

Plant and light

Palms and specimens go in first, then shrubs, color and lighting, timed for the best planting season.

Tropical Landscape FAQ

Questions About Lush Yards

Which palms grow best in the Phoenix area?

Mediterranean fan palms, date palms and Mexican fan palms are reliable in Valley heat and soil. Queen palms are popular but often struggle in alkaline soil. We choose palms by mature size, how clean they are near a pool and the look you want.

Will a tropical landscape use a lot of water?

More than a desert design, but less than you might think. Zoned drip irrigation, mulch and keeping lush planting near the pool keep water use under control, while tougher plants near the walls carry the look without the thirst.

What happens to tropical plants in a freeze?

Hard freezes are uncommon in the East Valley, but frost-tender plants can lose leaves or die back on the coldest nights, and most recover in spring. Placement near warm walls and covering tender plants before a frost reduce damage.

How do I keep palm fronds and flowers out of the pool?

Plant choice and distance. We keep heavy shedders back from the water and plan regular palm trimming. Our maintenance team can keep both the pool and the planting tidy.

Can a tropical oasis work on a small lot?

Yes. A few well-placed palms, a lush border and one flowering wall can make a compact yard feel like a resort. Fewer, larger plants usually read better than many small ones.
