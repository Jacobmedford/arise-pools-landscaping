Outdoor Living

Your Second Living Room

In Arizona the backyard is a second living room for much of the year. We design the structures that make it comfortable: shade overhead, a place to cook, somewhere warm to gather and a screen for the game.

We plan outdoor living the way an architect plans a house, in rooms. A dining room near the kitchen, a lounge facing the pool, a fire room for winter evenings and a play zone on the lawn. Each gets its own surface, shade and lighting, and the paths between them are as considered as hallways.

Shade drives most of it. A solid-roof ramada blocks summer sun and rain, a pergola filters light and can take a louvered or retractable cover, and orientation decides which side needs protection from the low western sun. Our guide to backyard shade in Arizona walks through the options.

Everything is designed with the pool and landscape, so the yard reads as one continuous environment rather than a collection of add-ons. Gas, power and water are planned before the deck is poured, which is what makes a kitchen, a fire table or a TV look built in.

Built for Arizona

Comfort in Every Season

Arizona outdoor living swings from scorching afternoons to chilly nights. The design has to handle both.

Summer sun

Shade where it matters

The low afternoon sun from the west is the hardest to block. Ramadas, screens and trees on that side, plus light-colored paving, keep a patio usable into the evening.

Winter nights

Fire and warmth

Desert evenings from late fall into early spring are made for fire. A fire pit or fireplace placed out of the wind turns the cool months into prime outdoor season.

Monsoon

Wind and water

Structures are engineered for monsoon gusts, and roofs drain away from seating and the pool. Fabric and furniture choices account for sudden storms and dust.

UV

Materials that last

Desert sun fades and cracks finishes that hold up elsewhere. We choose counters, cabinetry, fabrics and finishes suited to intense sun and heat.

Outdoor Living FAQ

Questions About Outdoor Rooms

Should I choose a ramada or a pergola?

A ramada has a solid roof, so it gives full shade and keeps rain off furniture and electronics. A pergola filters light and feels more open, and it can take a louvered or retractable cover. Many yards use both: a ramada for the kitchen and TV, a pergola over a lounge.

Do outdoor living structures need permits?

Many do. Roofed structures, gas lines and electrical work typically need a city permit, and HOAs often review height, color and placement. We handle the drawings, engineering and submittals.

How do you keep an outdoor space cool in summer?

Shade first, then air movement, then water. Solid roofs and west-side screening block the worst sun, ceiling fans keep air moving, and misting cools the air in dry heat. Lighter paving stays cooler underfoot.

Can I put a TV outside in Arizona?

Yes, with the right setup. A weather-rated outdoor TV under a solid roof, out of direct sun and glare, holds up well. Power and signal lines are planned during construction so nothing is strung along the walls.

Can outdoor living be added after the pool is built?

Yes, though it is easiest when planned together so utilities run before decking is poured. If your pool is already in, we design around it and route gas and power with as little disruption to existing hardscape as possible.
