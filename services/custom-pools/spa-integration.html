Spa Integration

One Body of Water, Two Temperatures

In Arizona the spa often gets more use than the pool from November through March. Designing the two together puts the spa in the right place, at the right height and on the same equipment.

Placement comes first. A spa close to the back door is easy to reach on a cold January night, and one turned toward a view or a fire feature becomes the evening gathering spot. We also watch the sunset line, since a west-facing spa can be glorious in winter and brutal in August.

Height is the next decision. A raised spa faced in stone or tile adds the sound of a spillover and gives the yard a focal point. A flush spa keeps sight lines open and suits minimal, modern layouts. Either way, the spillover keeps spa water circulating with the pool so the chemistry stays shared.

Underneath, pool and spa share filtration, heating and Pentair automation, with valves that switch between pool and spa modes. That is what lets you start the spa from your phone on the drive home and step into hot water when you arrive. Read more about smart automation.

Design Details

What Makes a Great Spa

01

The spillover

A sheet of water from spa to pool adds sound and motion. Tile, stone or a sheer lip each give it a different character.

02

Seating layout

Benches at different depths and a lounge seat let tall and short guests soak comfortably, with jets where backs actually rest.

03

Facing material

Stacked stone feels warm and natural, glass mosaic shimmers at night and smooth tile reads modern. The facing sets the spa's personality.

04

Steps and handholds

Wide, lit steps and a coping you can grip make getting in and out easy, especially after dark.

05

Heat on demand

A heater sized for the spa brings it up to temperature quickly, so a spontaneous soak does not need hours of planning.

06

Light and fire

Spa lighting and a nearby fire bowl or fire pit turn a winter soak into the best part of the evening.

Built for Arizona

Hot Summers and Cool Desert Nights

An Arizona spa has to earn its place in July and in January.

Winter use

Spa season is real

Desert winter nights get cold enough that a hot spa becomes the main event. A fast heater, a cover where practical and a short, lit path from the house make winter soaking easy.

Summer use

A spa that cools off

In summer many owners turn the heat off and use the spa as a shaded, cool soaking pool. Placing it under or near shade stretches its season.

Monsoon

Debris in the spillway

Monsoon wind blows dust and leaves into a raised spa first. Good skimming, a spillway that is easy to clean and a post-storm routine keep both bodies of water clear.

Hard water

Calcium on the spill edge

Water sheeting over a spillway leaves calcium as it evaporates. Smooth tile on the spill edge and balanced chemistry keep it from building into a white crust.

Spa FAQ

Questions About Pool and Spa Pairs

Should my spa be raised or flush with the pool?

A raised spa adds a focal point and the sound of a spillover, and it keeps pool splash out of the spa. A flush spa keeps sight lines open and suits modern layouts. Your view, the house style and how the yard is used usually decide it.

How fast can an attached spa heat up?

It depends on spa size, the heater and the starting water temperature, but an attached spa with a properly sized heater warms far faster than a whole pool. Pentair automation lets you start it from your phone before you get home.

Can I add a spa to my existing pool?

Often, yes. A spa can sometimes be built into an existing pool or added beside it with a spillover, provided the structure, plumbing and equipment pad can support it. Our remodel team evaluates that on site.

Does an attached spa share water with the pool?

Yes. The spillover returns spa water to the pool, and both run through the same filtration and sanitizing system. In spa mode, valves isolate the spa so it heats quickly.

Is a separate hot tub a better choice?

A portable hot tub can go anywhere, but it rarely matches the pool and deck. An integrated spa is built from the same materials, shares equipment and reads as part of the design.
