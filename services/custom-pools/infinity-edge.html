Vanishing-Edge Design

Water That Seems to Keep Going

An infinity edge lowers one wall of the pool so water slips over it into a hidden basin below. From the right vantage point, the pool appears to run straight into the view.

That illusion depends on geometry. We find the spot where you will most often see the pool, usually the living room, a covered patio or a seat at the outdoor kitchen, and set the height and line of the edge from there. Move that vantage point a few feet and the effect changes, so it gets decided early.

Behind the edge sits the working part: a catch basin sized for the water that spills over plus the surge when people get in. A dedicated pump returns water to the pool, and an automatic fill keeps the level steady as desert air pulls water away. Done well, you only ever notice the edge.

Hillside lots in Paradise Valley, Fountain Hills, Cave Creek and north Scottsdale are the classic setting, but a slope is not always required. A raised edge facing open desert or a mountain line can deliver a similar effect on a flatter lot. Our guide to infinity edges on hillside lots goes deeper.

Infinity-Edge Essentials
  • Vantage-point planning from inside the house
  • Engineered walls for sloped lots
  • Catch basin sized for surge and evaporation
  • Edge pump on a Pentair automation schedule
  • Automatic water fill

Edge Options

Ways to Make Water Disappear

01

Single vanishing edge

One wall faces the view and spills into a basin below, the classic choice when the view runs in one direction.

02

Corner edges

Two adjoining walls disappear, which suits the corner of a hillside lot with views across two sides.

03

Perimeter overflow

Water spills over every edge into a surrounding grate, so the surface sits flush with the deck like a mirror.

04

The spill wall

Tile, stone or a finish that matches the pool. Tile reads crisp and cleans easily where water flows constantly.

05

Basin placement

Tucked below a slope, hidden behind planting, or finished as a narrow reflecting channel you are meant to see.

06

Night lighting

Lights in the pool and along the spill wall make the edge glow after sunset, when the yard gets the most use.

Built for Arizona

What the Desert Asks of an Edge

A vanishing edge exposes more moving water to sun, wind and dust than any other pool type. Design for that and it stays effortless.

Evaporation

More water in motion

Water flowing over an edge loses more to dry desert air than a still surface. An automatic fill tops up the basin, and running the edge on a schedule keeps water use in check.

Wind and dust

Monsoon gusts

Strong monsoon winds can push water over the edge and blow dust into the basin. Basin sizing and a post-storm clean-out keep it running. See our monsoon checklist.

Slope

Hillside structure

Hillside pools often need deeper footings, engineered walls and careful drainage. Some towns review hillside work closely, including Paradise Valley and the sensitive desert areas of Scottsdale.

Hard water

Scale on the spill wall

Constant flow and evaporation leave calcium on the edge faster than on a still pool. Smooth tile, steady chemistry and regular brushing keep it clean.

Infinity-Edge FAQ

Questions About Vanishing Edges

Do I need a hillside lot for an infinity-edge pool?

No, though a slope makes the effect easiest. On flatter lots we can raise the edge to face open space or use a perimeter overflow so the water sits flush with the deck. What matters is a view worth framing and a vantage point that lines up with it.

Why does an infinity edge cost more than a standard pool?

It adds structure: the spill wall, the catch basin, extra plumbing, a dedicated pump and, on hillsides, engineered walls. The amount depends on edge length and site conditions, so we price it specifically after the site visit.

Is an infinity-edge pool hard to maintain?

It asks a little more than a standard pool. The basin needs occasional cleaning, the spill wall needs brushing to keep calcium off, and chemistry matters more because the water is always moving. Automation and our maintenance service handle most of it.

Does an infinity edge use more water in Arizona?

Yes. Moving water evaporates faster, especially in summer heat and wind. Running the edge on a schedule rather than around the clock, and pausing it during monsoon wind, keeps water use reasonable while the automatic fill holds the level.

Can an infinity edge be added to an existing pool?

Occasionally, if the pool faces the view and the site allows a catch basin. It is a major structural change, so we evaluate the shell, grade and plumbing on site before recommending it.
