Landscape Design

A Pool Is Only as Good as the World Around It

Planting, grade, lighting and irrigation decide whether a backyard feels finished. Our landscape designers work alongside the pool team from the first sketch, so the yard reads as one composition.

We start with structure: where shade is needed, which views to frame and which to screen, and how people move from the house to the water, the lawn and the side gate. Trees and larger shrubs go on the plan first because they shape everything else, from deck temperature to privacy from a two-story neighbor.

Then the palette. Desert-modern designs lean on sculptural agaves, native shrubs and decomposed granite. Tropical oasis designs layer palms and flowering color for a resort feel. Many East Valley yards land in between, with a lush pool zone near the house that steps down to low-water planting toward the walls.

Every plan includes drip irrigation zoned by plant type, landscape lighting and a realistic maintenance picture, because the best landscape still looks right three summers later. Our guide to designing for monsoon, dust and summer heat covers the climate side in depth.

Built for Arizona

Plants and Soil in the Desert

Arizona landscapes succeed or fail on a handful of conditions most national guides ignore.

Caliche

Planting through hardpan

Caliche can trap water around roots and drown plants in the middle of a desert. We break through it or drill drainage for trees and larger shrubs so roots can grow down.

Heat

The right plant, the right wall

A plant that thrives on an east wall can scorch on a west wall. Placement by exposure, plus fall planting so roots establish before summer, makes the difference.

Monsoon

Storms and runoff

Monsoon downpours move soil, mulch and gravel fast. Grading, rock-lined dry washes and swales send water where it can soak in, away from the pool and the house.

Frost

Cold desert nights

Frost-tender favorites like bougainvillea can be damaged on the coldest winter nights. We place them near walls that hold warmth and mix in hardier plants.

The Process

How a Landscape Comes Together

Site and sun study

At the site visit we note exposure, soil, drainage, existing trees and the views to keep or hide.

Planting and lighting plan

Renderings show plants, lighting and materials in place, and a detailed proposal follows within a week.

Grade and irrigate

Grades are set and drip lines are run by zone before plants and rock go in.

Plant and refine

Trees and specimens go in first, then shrubs, groundcover, rock and lighting, adjusted on site.

Landscape FAQ

Questions About Arizona Landscapes

When is the best time to plant in the Phoenix area?

Fall suits most desert and adapted plants, because roots establish through the mild winter before summer heat arrives. Spring planting works with more careful watering. We schedule installation around the plants in your design.

Can I have a lush yard and still save water?

Yes. Concentrate lush planting where you will see and enjoy it, usually near the pool and patio, and use desert-adapted plants farther out. Drip irrigation zoned by plant type keeps water use efficient.

Which plants should I keep away from my pool?

Anything that drops heavy litter, and anything with sharp spines near walkways. Palo verde blossoms, mesquite pods and bougainvillea bracts all end up in the water if planted too close, so we keep them at a distance.

Is synthetic turf a good idea in Arizona?

For many families, yes. Turf stays green without irrigation and handles play and pets, though it gets hot in direct summer sun. We place it where shade helps or where it is used mornings and evenings.

Do you design landscape lighting too?

Yes. Lighting is part of every landscape plan: uplighting for trees and specimens, path lights for safe walking and soft wall wash for depth. Wiring is planned with the planting so fixtures sit cleanly in the beds.
