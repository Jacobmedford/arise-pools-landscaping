Outdoor Kitchens

Cook Outside, Stay With Your Guests

An outdoor kitchen moves the cook into the party. Instead of disappearing indoors, whoever is at the grill can see the pool, talk with friends and keep an eye on the kids.

We design the kitchen around how you actually cook. Some clients want a serious grill station with side burners, a smoker and plenty of prep counter. Others want a bar with seating, a refrigerator and a sink for drinks, with the grill off to one side. The layout, a straight run, an L or an island with stools, follows that answer.

Placement matters as much as the appliances. A kitchen close to the house shortens trips for ingredients and simplifies utility runs. Smoke should drift away from seating and the pool, and the cook should face the action, not a wall. Under a ramada or covered patio, the kitchen stays usable through summer afternoons and monsoon showers.

Gas, power and water are planned before the deck is poured, so the kitchen looks built in. It is part of our wider outdoor living work, which covers the shade, seating and fire features around it.

Kitchen Features
  • Built-in grills and side burners
  • Bars with seating
  • Outdoor-rated refrigeration and sinks
  • Stone, stucco or tile-clad islands
  • Counters suited to sun and heat
  • Cover from a ramada or pergola

Planning

A Kitchen That Gets Used

01

The grill zone

Counter space on both sides of the grill for landing plates, plus a burner or smoker if you cook beyond burgers.

02

The bar

A raised bar counter with stools lets guests face the cook, the pool or a TV, and keeps traffic out of the work area.

03

Cold storage

Outdoor-rated refrigerators and ice storage save the walk indoors, and shade helps them run efficiently in summer.

04

Water and cleanup

A sink makes prep and cleanup easy, and a trash pull-out keeps the counters clear.

05

Shade overhead

A ramada, pergola or covered patio protects the cook, the electronics and the countertops from the worst sun.

06

Light for cooking

Task lighting over the grill and counters, with softer light at the bar, makes evening cooking safe and pleasant.

Built for Arizona

Materials That Handle the Heat

Outdoor kitchens in the Valley face punishing sun, monsoon dust and cool winter nights.

Countertops

Surfaces that stay true

Some engineered stone can discolor or crack in direct desert sun. We favor granite, porcelain and other surfaces suited to UV and heat, and keep dark tops out of full afternoon sun.

Cabinetry

Built for weather

Stainless steel and masonry islands hold up to heat and moisture. Wood and laminates that work indoors tend to warp and fade outside.

Monsoon dust

Quick to clean

Dust storms coat everything. Sealed counters, closed storage and grill covers make post-storm cleanup fast.

Utilities

Gas and power done right

Gas lines, electrical circuits and water supply are permitted and run underground before the deck goes in, with shutoffs placed where you can reach them.

Outdoor Kitchen FAQ

Questions About Cooking Outside

Where should an outdoor kitchen go?

Usually close to the house for easy trips and shorter utility runs, under shade, and turned so the cook faces the pool or seating. We also watch prevailing wind so smoke drifts away from guests.

Does an outdoor kitchen need a permit?

If it includes gas, electrical or plumbing, plan on a permit, and many HOAs review structures and shade covers. We handle the drawings and submittals as part of the project.

What countertop is best for an outdoor kitchen in Arizona?

Granite and porcelain are strong choices because they handle UV and heat, while many engineered quartz products are not meant for direct sun. Lighter colors stay cooler to the touch.

Should my outdoor kitchen be covered?

In Arizona, almost always. A ramada, pergola or covered patio protects the cook from summer sun, keeps rain and dust off appliances and makes the space usable for more of the year.

Can you add an outdoor kitchen to my existing patio?

Often, yes. We check the existing slab, the route for gas and power and the room for seating, then design the kitchen to fit. Sometimes extending the patio gives the kitchen and bar the space they need.
