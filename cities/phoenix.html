Pools & Landscapes in Phoenix

Building a Pool in Phoenix

Phoenix is big enough that a pool in Arcadia and a pool in Desert Ridge have little in common. Arcadia, at the south foot of Camelback Mountain, began as citrus groves watered by the Arcadia Water Company, founded in 1919. Ahwatukee Foothills follows the edge of South Mountain Park, and Desert Ridge is a 5,700-acre master plan in northeast Phoenix. We start every design by reading the lot in front of us.

The City of Phoenix Planning & Development Department handles residential pool and spa permits, and the process can be completed online. Reviews take longer when a pool sits on a hillside, uses a custom plan or adds structural elements such as water features, so we flag those early and build the time into your schedule.

Hillside lots carry their own rules. The city adopted its Hillside Ordinance in 1972 to limit grading and scarring on desert slopes, and it caps how much of a hillside lot can be disturbed. Those limits decide where a pool, deck and retaining walls can go.

Design Notes

What Phoenix Yards Ask For

Three situations we plan for across the city.

01

Irrigated Lots

SRP still delivers flood irrigation to parts of the Valley, including some older Phoenix neighborhoods like Arcadia. On an irrigated lot we set pool and deck elevations, berms and drainage so irrigation water stays out of the pool and away from the equipment.

02

Mature Citrus

Many Arcadia lots still hold old citrus trees. We design around the healthiest ones and often let a grove become the backdrop for a fire pit or a dining terrace, the way the citrus row frames the fire pit at Family Focused.

03

Foothill Slopes

Along South Mountain in Ahwatukee and in the north Phoenix foothills, grade changes bring engineering, retaining walls and drainage into the plan. A raised spa or a terraced deck can turn the slope into the best seat in the yard.

Good Questions

Questions From Phoenix Homeowners

How do pool permits work in Phoenix?

The City of Phoenix Planning & Development Department issues residential pool and spa permits, and submittals can be handled online. We prepare the drawings and engineering, submit them and coordinate inspections during the build.

Does a hillside lot change the process?

Yes. Phoenix reviews take longer for hillside pools, and the Hillside Ordinance limits how much of a hillside lot can be graded or built on. We map those limits during design so the pool, deck and walls fit within them.

Can you build a pool on a flood-irrigated Arcadia lot?

Yes, with the right grading. We set elevations, berms and drainage so irrigation water flows around the pool area, keep the equipment pad high and dry, and plan the barrier and gates to work with the irrigation layout.

Can you save my mature citrus trees?

Often. We place the pool and footings with root zones in mind, protect trees during construction where we can and give you an honest read on which ones are worth keeping.

Do you work in Ahwatukee and Desert Ridge?

Yes. Ahwatukee sits along South Mountain on the Chandler and Tempe side of the city, and Desert Ridge is in northeast Phoenix. If your home is in an HOA, we handle that review alongside the city permit.
