Pools & Landscapes in Cave Creek

Building a Pool in Cave Creek

Cave Creek's town limits climb from about 1,800 feet to more than 3,900 feet, and its historic core sits at the base of Black Mountain. Many lots here are desert first and yard second, with washes, boulders and native plants the Town expects you to keep. A pool in that setting should look like it settled into the land.

Start by checking which jurisdiction you are in. The Town of Cave Creek issues pool and pool barrier permits through its online portal, and pools are permitted separately from other building work. Some neighborhoods with Cave Creek mailing addresses, including Tatum Ranch, sit inside the City of Phoenix, which means a Phoenix permit instead. We confirm the right office before design gets far.

Inside town limits, the zoning code requires indigenous plants on new construction and building permits, and grading work can call for a native plant inventory. On hillside lots, exposed cuts must sit behind retaining walls or under natural rock veneer treated to look weathered. Those rules shape the design as much as the house does.

Design Notes

What Cave Creek Yards Ask For

Desert character comes first in the foothills.

01

Design Around the Desert

We walk the lot and mark the saguaros, palo verdes, boulders and washes worth keeping before the pool has a shape. The design then threads between them, so the yard keeps the character that brought you to Cave Creek.

02

Cut Into Hills Carefully

Hillside pools mean cuts into the slope. The Town wants those cuts hidden behind retaining walls or natural rock veneer aged to match, so we plan the stonework into the design and the proposal from the start.

03

A Native Palette

The landscaping ordinance calls for indigenous plants on new work. We build the planting plan around native trees, shrubs and cacti and use stone, gravel and boulders for texture near the water, much like the dry washes at The Desert Escape.

Foothill homes in town and the communities around it.

Tatum Ranch

A 1,500-acre community begun in 1989 with a Cave Creek mailing address, listed by the City of Phoenix as one of its neighborhood associations. Permits here run through Phoenix.

Homes around the 3,398-foot peak that anchors the town core. Slopes, rock and long views toward Carefree and the Valley make terraced designs a natural choice.

The wider foothills region shared by Cave Creek and Carefree, known for open Sonoran desert. Larger desert lots invite pools that sit low and blend in.

Good Questions

Questions From Cave Creek Homeowners

Is my Cave Creek address in the Town of Cave Creek?

Not always. Some neighborhoods with Cave Creek mailing addresses, Tatum Ranch among them, are inside the City of Phoenix. Your jurisdiction decides who issues the permit and which landscaping rules apply, so we confirm it first.

Who issues pool permits in the Town of Cave Creek?

The Town's Building Safety office, through its online permitting portal. Pools and spas are permitted separately, and the pool barrier must meet Arizona's enclosure law. We prepare the submittal and handle it for you.

Do I have to use native plants?

Inside town limits, the landscaping ordinance requires indigenous plants for new construction and building permits, and grading can require a native plant inventory. We design the planting plan to comply and to look like the desert around it.

Can you build on a steep foothill lot?

Yes, with engineering and stonework. The Town's hillside rules require exposed cuts to sit behind retaining walls or under natural rock veneer treated to look aged, and we design those walls as part of the yard.

Do you build pools for Tatum Ranch homes?

Yes. Because Tatum Ranch sits inside Phoenix, permits go through the City of Phoenix Planning & Development Department, and we check the Tatum Ranch Community Association's requirements before submitting.
