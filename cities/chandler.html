Pools & Landscapes in Chandler

Designing a Pool in Chandler

Chandler sits just southwest of our Gilbert office, and its southern half is a run of master-planned communities built around water. Many homes in Ocotillo and Fulton Ranch back onto man-made lakes, and Layton Lakes straddles the Chandler and Gilbert line with its own streams and waterfalls. A pool in one of those yards looks best when it reads as part of the water around it.

The City of Chandler's Building Safety division permits pools and spas, and the city is specific about placement. A pool has to sit in the backyard, never the front, and the water's edge must stay at least five feet from any side or back property line. On a narrow lot that rule shapes the design from the first sketch, so we measure before we draw.

Most of these neighborhoods also have an association with its own architectural review. We build the HOA package and the city submittal from one site plan, so neither waits on the other.

Design Notes

What Chandler Yards Ask For

Lake views, tight side yards and long warm evenings shape most of our Chandler designs.

01

Frame the Lake

On a lakefront lot the best view is already there. We keep the pool low and the deck open toward the water, use view fencing where the community allows it and pick an interior finish that picks up the lake's color at dusk.

02

Work With the Five-Foot Rule

Because the water has to stay five feet off the side and rear lines, long rectangles and L-shapes often fit better than a wide freeform. A raised spa or a sheer wall at the far end adds height and sound without eating into the setback.

03

Shade Beside the Water

Chandler summers push swimming into the mornings and evenings. We set pergolas and covered seating next to the pool rather than over it, so the water gets sun and the adults get cover a few steps away. Our shade guide explains the orientation.

Where We Work

Chandler Neighborhoods We Serve

From the lakes of south Chandler to the foothills near Hunt Highway.

Ocotillo

A south Chandler master plan known for its man-made lakes and golf. Lakefront yards here reward low profiles, open decks and lighting that stays off the water.

Fulton Ranch

A guard-gated lake community beside Ocotillo, built in the 2000s. Gated enclaves can add design guidelines of their own, so we read them before the first concept.

Layton Lakes

On the Chandler and Gilbert line near Lindsay and Queen Creek Roads, threaded with lakes, streams and waterfalls. Backyard water features can borrow that same language.

Sun Groves

About 600 acres in southeast Chandler at the foot of the San Tan Mountains. Mountain views to the south make sunset seating and a west-facing patio worth planning around.

Good Questions

Questions From Chandler Homeowners

Where can a pool go on a Chandler lot?

In the backyard. The City of Chandler does not allow pools in front yards, and the water's edge has to sit at least five feet from any side or back property line. We confirm the current rules for your address before the design is final.

Who permits pools in Chandler?

The City of Chandler's Building Safety division reviews the plans, issues the permit and inspects the work. We handle the engineering and submittal, and the barrier must also meet Arizona's five-foot enclosure law.

Do Ocotillo and Fulton Ranch require HOA approval?

Both are master-planned communities with associations, so plan on architectural review. Rules can differ by enclave, so we read your guidelines early and submit alongside the city permit.

Can a pool look good next to a lake?

Yes, when it defers to the view. We keep coping and walls low on the water side, use view fencing where it is allowed and aim lighting so it stays in your yard. The pool becomes the foreground and the lake stays the main event.

How long will my Chandler project take?

Permits and engineering usually run two to four weeks, then eight to fourteen weeks of construction with weekly updates. HOA calendars can add time, so we start that submittal during design.
