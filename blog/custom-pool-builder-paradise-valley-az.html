Custom Pool Builder in Paradise Valley, AZ: Ideas From The Hidden Jewel

October 31, 2026 · 6-minute read

Paradise Valley is one of the few Valley towns where the lot itself is part of the design brief. Many homes sit on large parcels, some on hillsides, and the views, the privacy and the evening light all matter before anyone talks about tile. If you are searching for a custom pool builder in Paradise Valley, AZ, this guide covers how we approach a yard like yours, with one of our own projects as the reference.

That project is The Hidden Jewel, a remodel we completed in Gilbert. It is not a Paradise Valley yard, and we will not pretend otherwise. We show it because the ideas carry over: a freeform pool, lighting built into the stone, a covered bar and a fire pit within a few steps of the back door.

The Hidden Jewel as a Design Reference

The Hidden Jewel in Gilbert turns inward. Walls and neighboring rooflines surround the backyard, so the design puts its energy into the pool, the bar and the glow after dark. The pool curves like a lagoon, with a shallow lounge area, a raised spa wrapped in blue mosaic tile and a curved stacked-stone wall sending sheer water into the pool.

A Paradise Valley lot is usually larger and more open than this one, which changes the question. Instead of asking how to fit everything in, you are deciding where each piece belongs and how far apart to place them. The same ingredients can become separate rooms across the property.

Start With the Lot, Not the Pool Shape

On a larger parcel, the first drawing is a site plan. Where does the house look out? Which direction does the afternoon sun come from? Is there a view worth framing, or a neighbor worth screening? Those answers place the pool, and the shape follows. Freeform curves tend to sit comfortably on open ground, while a straighter, more architectural pool can echo a modern home. Our pages on freeform and geometric pools show both directions.

If Your Lot Slopes

The town has hillside development regulations, and slope is often what decides what can be built on a lot. If your home is on a hillside, grading, retaining walls and approvals come before design details. Sloped sites also open up options like a raised spa or an infinity edge, and our post on infinity edge pools for Arizona hillside lots explains how those work.

Plan the Approvals Early

Every new pool needs a building permit. If your neighborhood has an HOA or its own review process, plan for that as well. The rules differ from lot to lot, so we confirm what applies to your address and start the paperwork while the design is still being refined. For a sense of the process, see our post on pool permits and HOA approval in Scottsdale.

Lighting Does the Heavy Lifting

On a big lot, nighttime is when the yard looks its best. At The Hidden Jewel, linear lights sit under the coping of the stone wall, the raised spa and the curved step-down benches, so the edges glow and every step is easy to read. Palms and bougainvillea along the back wall are uplit as well. Because the lights are set while the stone goes up, the wiring stays hidden and the lines stay clean.

In a town known for dark skies and quiet streets, softer lighting is usually the better call. Low, warm light on edges and plantings reads as luxury. Bright floods do not. We build with Pentair equipment, so scenes, spa heat and pump schedules can be run from an app. Our smart automation page has the details.

Build the Outdoor Rooms

The bar at The Hidden Jewel is its own covered structure, with a slatted ceiling, recessed lights, a ceiling fan, upholstered stools and a wall-mounted TV. A built-in stainless grill and side burner sit under the same roof. Across the deck, a square fire pit with low lounge chairs sits beside the lit pool steps.

With more room to work with, a Paradise Valley yard can spread these pieces out: a kitchen near the house, a fire pit at the far edge of the lawn, a spa that faces the view. Our outdoor living and water and fire features teams plan them together, and outdoor kitchens are a common starting point. Paving and walls come from our hardscape work, and landscape design ties the planting to the pool.

Remodel or Build New

Established homes often already have a pool, and a remodel can change the whole feel of a yard. The Hidden Jewel is one, with new paving, lit benches, a mosaic spa and a refreshed front yard. Whether that fits your property depends on the shell, the plumbing and how far the new layout departs from the old one. We assess that on site. Our pool remodels page covers the scope.

On budget, Phoenix-metro pricing varies widely. A new pool with a spa, lighting and outdoor living typically runs well into six figures, and larger or sloped lots can add to the total. Our breakdown of what a $125k pool actually gets you shows what drives the number. Construction usually takes eight to fourteen weeks once design and approvals are done.

Paradise Valley Pool Questions, Answered

Do I need a permit to build a pool in Paradise Valley?
Yes. A new pool needs a building permit from the town, and many neighborhoods also have architectural review. Requirements vary by lot, so we confirm the current rules for your address and handle the submittals.
Do hillside lot rules affect a Paradise Valley pool?
They can. The town has hillside development regulations, and slope often shapes what can be built. If your lot is on a hillside, we check the rules and the grading early, before the design is set.
What does a custom pool cost in Paradise Valley?
It depends on scope and site. In the Phoenix metro, a new pool with a spa, lighting and outdoor living typically runs well into six figures, and larger or sloped lots can add to that. A design consultation gives you a real number.
How long does a custom pool take to build?
Most projects run eight to fourteen weeks of construction, after design and approvals are complete. Your timeline depends on scope, permits and weather.
