Custom Pool Builder in McCormick Ranch, Scottsdale: Lessons From Legacy Living

October 29, 2026 · 6-minute read

McCormick Ranch is one of the best-known neighborhoods in Scottsdale. It started as a working ranch owned by the McCormick family, and the planned community that followed has its property owners' association dating to 1972. Today the area is known for its golf courses, lakes and a network of greenbelts and bike paths. Homeowners who live there tend to value the setting, and a backyard should match it.

If you are searching for a custom pool builder in McCormick Ranch, Scottsdale, this guide covers what to think about before you start. We are based in San Tan Valley, so we use one of our own finished yards to show the thinking. It is called Legacy Living, and it sits on a much bigger lot than most of McCormick Ranch. That makes it a useful study, because the ideas scale down well.

Legacy Living in San Tan Valley

Legacy Living in San Tan Valley uses every part of its lot. The main house and a separate pool house face each other across a courtyard, and the pool sits between them so both buildings open onto the water. From there the yard steps out into zones: a lounge terrace, fire pit seating, a putting green and a lit pickleball court.

The pool has clean straight edges on one side and a soft curve on the other, with a sun shelf facing the pool house. A raised spa at one corner, wrapped in stacked stone, sheets over stepped blue glass tile into the pool. Palms frame the water and are uplit after dark.

What Makes a McCormick Ranch Yard Different

Most of the yards in McCormick Ranch sit inside an established neighborhood, with mature trees and neighbors close by. Listing data for the area suggests many lots are modest in size, though they vary a lot from street to street. Some back onto greenbelt or water, and some sit on a quiet cul-de-sac. We would rather measure your lot than guess from a map.

Three things tend to drive the design on a lot like this. The first is privacy, because mature neighborhoods put windows and rooflines close to the yard. The second is the existing landscape. The third is how the pool reads from inside the house. For shape ideas on tighter lots, see our guide to pool shapes for Arizona lots under a quarter acre, and our plunge pools and geometric designs are built for exactly these footprints.

Borrowing the Best Ideas From a Big Lot

Make the Deck Do Some Work

At Legacy Living, travertine pavers cover the courtyard. In places they open into a grid with turf ribbons between the stones, which softens a large deck and draws a path from the covered patio to the water. The same trick works on a smaller lot, where a strip of turf between pavers keeps the yard from reading as one slab of stone. Our hardscape team plans the grid so it lines up with the doors and the pool edge.

Give the Spa a Role

A raised spa is the most compact way to add sound and movement to a yard. The sheeting water at Legacy Living comes from one corner of the pool, and the same idea fits a small lot with a single wall. If you want more, our water and fire features can add scuppers, bubblers or a fire bowl. Read more about spa integration if you are weighing a spa against a larger pool.

Plan for Night

Desert evenings are when a Scottsdale yard gets used. Legacy Living has pool lights, uplit palms, step lights and court lights, all planned with the build so the wiring is hidden and the glow looks intentional. We build with Pentair equipment, so lighting scenes and spa heat can be set from an app. Our smart automation page has the details.

Do You Need a Court, a Putting Green or a Pool House?

Probably not, and most McCormick Ranch lots would not hold all three. Legacy Living has them because the lot allowed it and the family wanted them. The takeaway for your yard is the process. We start by asking how the family will use the space, then fit the pieces to the lot. For many Scottsdale homes that means a pool, a shaded seating area and an outdoor kitchen. For others it means a small sport court from our hardscape and sport courts work. Our outdoor living page shows other layouts.

Planting is part of the same decision. Legacy Living uses gravel beds with agaves, lantana and bird of paradise, which handle the heat well. A good landscape design also takes your neighbors' sight lines into account, using hedges and screens where you want privacy.

Approvals, Budget and Timeline

A new pool needs a building permit from the city, and an HOA or recorded design guidelines may add an architectural review. We have written a separate guide to pool permits and HOA approval in Scottsdale. Details vary by address, so we check yours before drawing anything final.

On budget, Phoenix-metro pricing varies with scope. A new pool with a spa, lighting and outdoor living typically runs well into six figures, and our post on what a $125k pool actually gets you breaks down what moves the number. If you already own a pool that needs an update, a remodel can change the yard without starting over. Construction usually takes eight to fourteen weeks once design and approvals are done.

McCormick Ranch Pool Questions, Answered

Do I need approval before building a pool in McCormick Ranch?
A new pool needs a City of Scottsdale building permit. If your home is in an HOA or has recorded design guidelines, plan on an architectural review as well. We confirm the current requirements for your address and handle the submittals.
Can a pool fit on a smaller McCormick Ranch lot?
Often, yes. Plunge pools, geometric lap shapes and freeform layouts with a raised spa are all designed for tighter yards. A site visit shows what your lot will hold.
What does a custom pool cost in Scottsdale?
It depends on scope. In the Phoenix metro, refreshes sit at the lower end and a new pool with a spa, lighting and outdoor living typically runs well into six figures. A design consultation gives you a real number for your yard.
How long does a custom pool take to build?
Most custom projects take eight to fourteen weeks of construction, after design and approvals are complete. Your timeline depends on scope and permit review.
