Custom Pool Builder in Scottsdale, AZ: Inside The Desert Escape

October 27, 2026 · 6-minute read

Scottsdale homeowners tend to arrive with a specific picture: a long pool, stone that looks like it grew there, and a mountain on the horizon. If you are searching for a custom pool builder in Scottsdale, AZ, this guide covers what that picture takes to build, using one of our own projects as the example.

We are based in San Tan Valley and work across the Valley, including Scottsdale. The project below sits on a desert lot below the Superstition Mountains, not in Scottsdale itself. We chose it because its ideas carry well to any lot with a view, from the McDowell Mountains to a quiet cul-de-sac. We call it The Desert Escape.

The Desert Escape in Superstition Mountains

The Desert Escape in Superstition Mountains is built around one sight line. The pool runs long and straight off the back of the house, lined up with the mountain skyline, and the rest of the yard is placed to keep that view open. Three bronze scuppers pour from a raised stacked-stone wall into the water.

The photos are best at sunset. Pool lights come on, the pillars along the perimeter wall glow, and the saguaros turn to silhouettes. That is the moment a view-lot design earns its keep.

Start With the View, Then Place the Water

Before we sketch a shape, we stand where you will stand. We look from the kitchen window, the patio table and the end of the pool, and we note what you want to see and what you would rather screen. Neighbors, a side wall and a utility box all count.

Rectangular and geometric pools suit this approach, because a straight edge points at something. Our geometric pool designs are a good starting point if you like the clean lines of a desert-modern home. If your lot slopes toward the view, an infinity edge is worth a look, and our post on infinity edge pools for hillside lots explains when it works.

One Stone, Used Everywhere

Travertine does most of the talking at The Desert Escape. The same warm, multi-tone stone, laid in a mixed-size pattern, covers the pool deck, the long patio along the house, the gate steps and the kitchen counters. Stacked stone handles the vertical work on the raised pool wall, the kitchen and the pillars of the perimeter fence.

Keeping the palette short is what makes a yard feel calm. When the deck, steps and counters match, the eye stops cataloguing materials and goes to the mountains. Our hardscape team plans the stone, grades and drainage together with the pool so seams and levels line up.

Build the Kitchen Into the Landscape

At this property the outdoor kitchen is wrapped in the same stacked stone as the pool wall. It holds a stainless grill, stainless doors and drawers, and a pizza oven on the counter, with bar seating facing the water. The kitchen looks out at the mountain, so whoever is cooking never turns their back on the view.

Scottsdale summers push most outdoor cooking to the evening, so shade and lighting matter as much as the grill. Our outdoor kitchens are drawn together with the patio cover, and the covered patio here adds a wood-plank ceiling with a fan and recessed lights, a wicker lounge set and a hot tub in the corner. If you want that kind of spa, see our notes on spa integration. For the full range of outdoor rooms, start at outdoor living.

Keep the Desert at the Edges

Past the wall, the landscape at The Desert Escape stays desert. Rock-lined dry washes and gravel paths run between saguaros, prickly pear and round-clipped shrubs, and an iron gate between lit stone pillars opens onto travertine steps. The iron panels keep the view open while the stone gives the yard a boundary.

That restraint is practical. Native plantings need little water once established, and they make a pool read as an oasis against a natural backdrop. Our desert modern landscape design work follows the same logic, and the broader landscape design page shows other directions.

Approvals, Budget and Timing in Scottsdale

Every new pool needs a building permit, and homes in an HOA typically need architectural review before construction. If your home is in an HOA, ask early about wall height, lighting and color rules. We walk through the general process in pool permits and HOA approval in Scottsdale. Requirements vary by address, so we confirm them for each project.

On budget, Phoenix-metro pricing depends heavily on scope. A new pool with a spa, lighting, travertine hardscape and an outdoor kitchen typically runs well into six figures. Our post on what a $125k pool actually gets you breaks down where the money goes. Once designs and permits are in place, construction typically takes eight to fourteen weeks.

One practical note for desert lots: plan for monsoon wind and dust from the start. Our guide to designing for monsoon, dust and 115 degree summers covers what to build in.

Scottsdale Pool Questions, Answered

Do I need a permit to build a pool in Scottsdale?
Yes. A new pool needs a building permit, and homes in an HOA usually need architectural approval as well. We confirm the current requirements for your address and handle the submittals as part of the project.
Can you design a pool around a mountain view?
Yes. The Desert Escape was laid out so the pool lines up with the Superstition skyline. On any lot with a view, we start by locating the sight lines from the patio and the back door, then place the water, walls and seating to keep them open.
What does a custom pool with an outdoor kitchen cost in Scottsdale?
It depends on scope. In the Phoenix metro, a new pool with a spa, lighting and an outdoor kitchen typically runs well into six figures. A design consultation gives you a real number for your lot.
How long does a custom pool project take?
Construction typically runs eight to fourteen weeks once designs are approved and permits are in hand. Approvals add time before that, and they vary by city and HOA.
