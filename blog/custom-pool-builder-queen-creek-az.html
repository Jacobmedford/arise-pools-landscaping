Custom Pool Builder in Queen Creek, AZ: Inside The Desert Escape

October 17, 2026 · 6-minute read

Queen Creek sits just down the road from our San Tan Valley base, so a lot of the yards we get asked about here are ones we already know by sight: wide lots, long views toward the San Tan Mountains, and plenty of room to build something with real range.

This is a guide to what a custom pool project in Queen Creek usually involves. To make it concrete, we use one of our own builds as the example, The Desert Escape. It sits on a desert lot below the Superstition Mountains rather than in Queen Creek itself, but the design questions it answers are the same ones Queen Creek homeowners ask.

Building a Pool in Queen Creek

The Town of Queen Creek incorporated in 1989, and its roots are agricultural. The Town's own history describes founding families drawn to the area in the early 1900s to farm cotton, corn and potatoes in the valley below the San Tan Mountains. That open, spread-out character still shapes how many homes sit on their lots.

Every pool needs a plan that fits the town and the lot. The Town of Queen Creek requires a permit for swimming pools and spas, and it lists several ways to meet the barrier rules. A five-foot fence on all sides works. So does a five-foot fence on three sides paired with a motorized safety cover or a self-closing, self-latching door, each with conditions. The site plan has to show the barrier and the gates. We settle on the approach early, because it affects where the pool sits and how the yard flows around it.

If your home is in a master-planned community, plan on an association review as well. Rules differ from one community to the next, so we check yours before the design is final and prepare the town and association submittals together. Our post on pool permits and HOA approval explains how that process generally works.

The Desert Escape, Our Example

The Desert Escape in Superstition Mountains is a backyard organized around one view. The pool runs long and straight off the back of the house, lined up with the Superstition skyline, and everything else is placed to keep that sight line open. Three bronze scuppers pour from a raised stacked-stone wall capped in travertine, and a tanning ledge edged in mosaic tile gives loungers a shallow place to sit in the water.

Queen Creek lots often have the same opportunity. If your yard faces the San Tan Mountains or open desert, the first question is where to stand and what to see. We walk the lot at the time of day you will use it, then place the pool, the seating and the shade to frame the view instead of block it.

One Material, Start to Finish

Travertine does most of the unifying work at The Desert Escape. The same warm, multi-tone stone covers the pool deck, the long patio along the house, the gate steps and the kitchen counters, so the hardscape reads as a single surface from the back door to the perimeter wall. Stacked stone handles the vertical work: the raised wall beside the pool, the outdoor kitchen and the pillars of the perimeter fence.

Repeating a short list of materials makes a large yard feel calm, and lighter stone stays more comfortable underfoot than dark pavers on a Queen Creek summer afternoon. Our hardscape team plans decking, walls and steps together with the pool so grades and drainage agree.

Shade, Cooking and the Evening Hours

A pool is used most when people can be comfortable around it. The covered patio at The Desert Escape has a wood-plank ceiling, a ceiling fan and recessed lights, with a wicker lounge set and a hot tub tucked into the corner a few steps from the water. Next to it, the outdoor kitchen is built into stacked stone with a stainless grill, stainless doors and drawers, a pizza oven on the counter and bar seating beside the pool.

Cooking, swimming and sitting out after dark all happen within a few steps of one another. Our broader outdoor living work follows the same idea, and our post on shade ramadas and pergolas goes deeper on covering a patio.

Pool lights, lit pillar caps and lit steps take over once the sun goes down, and they are far easier to run while the walls are going up than afterward. We build with Pentair equipment, so lighting scenes and pump schedules can be handled from an app. See our smart automation page for the details.

Keeping the Desert at the Edges

At The Desert Escape, the landscape stays desert past the wall. Rock-lined dry washes, gravel paths and boulders run between saguaros, prickly pear and round-clipped shrubs, and an iron gate between lit stone pillars opens onto travertine steps that lead into it.

That approach suits Queen Creek too, where the farm-country backdrop and the mountains already do plenty of visual work. Our desert modern landscape design and landscape design services plan plantings and paths alongside the pool, and the water and fire features we add, like sheer descents or a fire bowl, give you sound and warmth without much extra footprint. If you are still choosing a shape, our guide to pool shapes is a good starting point.

Budget and Timeline

Pricing in the Phoenix metro varies widely with scope. A refresh sits at the low end, while a new pool with a kitchen, covered patio, lighting and hardscape typically runs well into six figures. Our post on what a $125k pool actually gets you breaks down where the money goes. Once permits and approvals are in hand, construction typically runs eight to fourteen weeks. Queen Creek Pool Questions, Answered

Do I need a permit to build a pool in Queen Creek?
Yes. The Town of Queen Creek requires a permit for swimming pools and spas, and the plans have to show a compliant barrier. If your home is in a master-planned community, the association usually reviews the design too. We prepare both from one set of plans.
What pool barrier options does Queen Creek allow?
The Town lists a five-foot fence on all sides, or a five-foot fence on three sides paired with a motorized safety cover or a self-closing, self-latching door, each with conditions. We confirm the current rules for your address and choose the approach that suits your yard.
How long does a custom pool take to build?
Construction typically runs eight to fourteen weeks once permits and approvals are in hand. Design and approvals come before that, and the schedule depends on scope.
What does a custom pool cost in Queen Creek?
It depends on scope. In the Phoenix metro, a new pool with outdoor living features typically runs well into six figures, and a simple refresh costs far less. A design consultation gives you a real number for your yard.
